Bounce Rate optimization for publishers (exit intent)

Published: Oct 5, 2014 •
#blog

Understanding bounce rate

First off, according to Google Analytics:

"Bounce Rate is the percentage of single-page sessions (i.e. sessions in which the person left your site from the entrance page without interacting with the page)."

An important caveat: GA actually counts a bounce as a session with a single "pixel download" (__utm.gif). Events also trigger pixel downloads by default, so firing a custom event after the pageview can disguise a real bounce. You can avoid that by using "non-interaction" events.

On a news site, a blog, or any site where ad impressions drive revenue, bounce rate can be a key indicator. If you haven't dug into how exit rate differs, give Exit Rate vs Bounce Rate a read.

Why do we care? Because simply reducing bounce rate (without losing sessions) directly increases overall pageviews. Yay, goals!

What is exit intent?

Exit intent is a way to detect a user moving their mouse toward the top of the browser in a way that suggests they are about to leave your site. Pretty simple, actually.

Targeting users with exit intent

Exit intent targeting is definitely not a new concept. Many e-commerce sites have used it for years, showing a popup or overlay with a discount right before you "bounce." More info at exitintentpopup.com.

There are even a few services that will implement it on your site for you, like OptKit, OptiMonk, Bounce Exchange, and ExitIntent.

Personally, I like to control and understand the JavaScript, and there's a really nice open-source implementation on GitHub: ouibounce.

Good idea for publishers?

This could all be tested on a site easily and without being invasive. The real question (for me) is how a news site or blog should use the tactic, and whether the impact would be worth the effort.

Either way, I plan to test a few different ways to entice visitors to click "one more time." Some options: breaking news, related stories, or a targeted newsletter signup.
