Two of today’s largest “media giants” have made big moves into the news world over the past month or so.

On May 12th, Facebook launched “Instant Articles” with several large news publishers: The New York Times, National Geographic, BuzzFeed, NBC News, The Atlantic, The Guardian, BBC News, Spiegel, and Bild.

On June 22nd, Google launched “News Lab” with a handful of lesser-known partners, as TechCrunch reported.

![Google News Lab](http://68.media.tumblr.com/eaa422b785387fe90a850b135852cc5d/tumblr_inline_nqekrtEGe21qzitrw_500.png)

What I find most interesting is how fundamentally different the two “competitors” are in their approach.

Facebook is trying to solve one very specific problem with news publishing: “News sites are slow.” I applaud them for being so focused, but I think it’s a bit short-sighted, since that problem could go away overnight as devices and browsers change. I’m not saying this won’t improve their platform. I just don’t see how it warrants all the fanfare.

Google, on the other hand, is taking a longer-term, more strategic approach. Paraphrasing (and putting words in Google’s mouth): “We don’t know yet which problems to solve first, so we’re focusing on the discovery process.”

Either way, I think these two approaches are big “tells” about the culture of each company, and I have an obvious opinion about which one will pan out in the long term.

My other observation is that Facebook clearly sees apps as the future of publishing, while Google is taking publishing on the web more seriously. Google is also getting real about tools for publishers, and about making data journalism something news consumers can finally experience. In my experience, never bet against the web. Just ask Microsoft.

That said, having worked on making our NBC News articles available to Facebook through a data feed, I do see the possibility that this could spawn a new format for news content syndication. But I may have already said too much…

I should also mention that Apple announced its “News” app on June 8th, so we’ll have to wait for that one to hit the App Store (meh). Wired has more on that one.

Would love to hear what you think.
